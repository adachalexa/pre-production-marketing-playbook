The through line · $90M–$250M budgets

At blockbuster scale, the opening weekend is the bet, so the campaign has to turn the release into an event.

All three films started marketing seven months or more before release, spent heavily, and gave audiences a reason to see the film in theaters. Opening big was not enough: each one also earned an A CinemaScore, which carried it well past opening weekend.

What executives should take away

  1. 01Start seven months out, or more.First trailers landed 206–380 days before release. The Odyssey put tickets on sale a full year early.
  2. 02Sell an event, not just a movie.Premium formats (53% of The Odyssey's opening, 45% of Sinners'), Barbenheimer and year-early presales made going a must.
  3. 03Plan marketing at half to one times the budget.Reported spend ran $55M–$150M, or 50–103% of production budgets.
  4. 04Protect the second weekend.All three earned an A CinemaScore and finished at 3.9–5.8× their opening domestically.

Playbook rule · Fan-made content (UGC)

Even $100M+ campaigns run on what fans make.

Paid media buys attention, but posts from real fans are what people trust, and they keep a film in the conversation after the ad money stops. Every blockbuster here got more reach from fans than it could have bought.

33%of The Odyssey's TikTok views came from fans with under 10,000 followers, more than from mega accounts (29%).
13Mpeople made their own Barbie posters with the Selfie Generator, turning ads into personal posts.
10.5Mfan-made videos and posts about Sinners in four months (unverified).

How to engage

  1. 1Build in something to share.A poster maker, a dress code (pink for Barbie) or a look (Sinners' grillz) gives fans a reason to post.
  2. 2Seed everyday fans, not just big accounts.Small creators drove a third of The Odyssey's TikTok views, and most of its creator videos weren't paid.
  3. 3Keep feeding fans after opening.Premieres, awards news and format re-releases gave fans new moments to post about.
