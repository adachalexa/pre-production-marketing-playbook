The through line · $750K–$25M budgets

On a small budget, the audience is the marketing department, so build campaigns fans want to share.

None of these films could outspend a studio tentpole. They won by starting with a community, giving fans something to do instead of just something to watch, and letting word of mouth carry them past a modest opening.

What executives should take away

  1. 01Start with a built-in community.Two of the three came from YouTube: Curry Barker's 1M+ subscribers and Kane Parsons' Backrooms series.
  2. 02Design participation, not impressions.A text line with Nikki, a sold-out product drop, the 'Unwritten' exit dance and in-universe ads gave fans something to share.
  3. 03Judge the run, not the opening.Obsession and Anyone But You finished near 15× their openings; Backrooms' fandom front-loaded it to 2.4×.
  4. 04A shorter runway can work.First trailers came 94–242 days out and presales just 16–59 days, far shorter than the blockbusters.

Playbook rule · Fan-made content (UGC)

On a small budget, fan content isn't extra marketing. It is the marketing.

These films had little or no paid media, so fans' videos were their reach. Both films that grew after opening weekend had a fan trend behind them, and it reached the young audiences that ads miss.

How to engage

  1. 1Give fans one thing to copy.A prop, a look, a song or a set that fits on a phone screen: the One Wish Willow, Nikki's makeup, the exit dance, the yellow rooms.
  2. 2Spot trends early and join in.Sony didn't start the 'Unwritten' dance, but it amplified it and brought in the cast and Natasha Bedingfield.
  3. 3Make it interactive.A text line, a product drop and hidden lore gave fans something to do, not just something to watch.
